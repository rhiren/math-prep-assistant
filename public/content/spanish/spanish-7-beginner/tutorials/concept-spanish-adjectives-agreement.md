# Adjectives and Agreement

## Hook

In English, the adjective usually stays the same: tall boy, tall girl, tall students. In Spanish, adjectives often change to match the noun.

## Big Idea

Spanish adjectives usually agree with the noun in gender and number. That means the ending can change for masculine, feminine, singular, and plural nouns.

Memory anchor: **The adjective follows the noun's signals.**

## Learn It

Many adjectives ending in `-o` change like this:

| Noun | Adjective |
|---|---|
| chico alto | tall boy |
| chica alta | tall girl |
| chicos altos | tall boys |
| chicas altas | tall girls |

Adjectives ending in `-e` often work for masculine and feminine:

- estudiante inteligente
- chica inteligente

To make adjectives plural, often add `s` if the adjective ends in a vowel and `es` if it ends in a consonant.

## Build The Model

Use this agreement check:

`noun gender -> noun number -> adjective ending`

Example:

`chicas -> feminine plural -> altas`

## Memory Anchors

- **Gender means masculine or feminine.**
- **Number means singular or plural.**
- **Many adjectives come after the noun in Spanish.**

## Common Traps

- Trap: Leaving every adjective in the masculine singular form.  
  Fix: Check the noun first.
- Trap: Thinking every adjective changes for gender.  
  Fix: Some adjectives, like many `-e` adjectives, do not change for gender.
- Trap: Forgetting plural endings.  
  Fix: If the noun is plural, the adjective usually becomes plural too.

## Pause And Think

- Why is `chica alta` different from `chico alto`?
- How would you describe two tall girls?
- Which part do you check first: noun or adjective?

## Apply It

Choose the matching adjective:

- la profesora ____. (alto / alta)
- los estudiantes ____. (inteligente / inteligentes)
- las amigas ____. (simpática / simpáticas)
