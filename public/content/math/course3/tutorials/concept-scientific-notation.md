# Scientific Notation

## The big idea

Scientific notation writes a number as:

`a × 10ⁿ`

The coefficient `a` must be at least `1` and less than `10`.

Examples:

- `4.2 × 10⁵`
- `7.03 × 10⁻⁸`
- `1 × 10⁰`

## Large numbers

For a large number, move the decimal left until the coefficient is between 1 and
10. The number of moves becomes a positive exponent.

`58,000 = 5.8 × 10⁴`

The decimal moved 4 places left.

## Small numbers

For a small decimal, move the decimal right until the coefficient is between 1
and 10. The number of moves becomes a negative exponent.

`0.00036 = 3.6 × 10⁻⁴`

The decimal moved 4 places right, so the exponent is `-4`.

## Standard form

To convert back:

- positive exponent: move the decimal right
- negative exponent: move the decimal left

`6.1 × 10³ = 6,100`

`6.1 × 10⁻³ = 0.0061`

## Comparing scientific notation

When numbers are in proper scientific notation:

1. Compare exponents first.
2. If the exponents match, compare coefficients.

`3 × 10⁷ > 9 × 10⁶` because `10⁷` is ten times larger than `10⁶`.

`7.2 × 10⁻⁴ > 6.8 × 10⁻⁴` because the exponents match and `7.2 > 6.8`.

## Common mistake

`42 × 10⁵` is not proper scientific notation because `42` is not less than
10.

Rewrite it:

`42 × 10⁵ = 4.2 × 10⁶`
