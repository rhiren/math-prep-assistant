# Congruent Figures on a Grid

## Big Idea

Congruent figures have the same size and the same shape. On a grid, that means corresponding side lengths and corresponding angle measures match.

A figure can slide, flip, or turn and still be congruent to the original. The coordinates may change, and the figure may face a new direction, but the measurements that define its size and shape stay the same.

## What To Notice

- Translations, reflections, and rotations make congruent images.
- Congruent figures can be in different places on the grid.
- Congruent figures can face different directions.
- Corresponding side lengths must match.
- Corresponding angle measures must match.
- A stretch or dilation with a scale factor other than 1 changes size, so it does not make a congruent image.

## Worked Example

Triangle A has side lengths 3, 4, and 5. Triangle B has side lengths 5, 3, and 4.

The order is different, but the three side lengths match: 3, 4, and 5.

If the corresponding angles also match, Triangle A and Triangle B are congruent. One triangle may have been translated, reflected, or rotated to land on the other.

## Strategy

| Question | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Are two grid figures congruent? | Match corresponding side lengths and angles. |
| Did a rigid transformation happen? | Translation, reflection, and rotation preserve congruence. |
| Did the figure stretch or scale? | Then it is not congruent unless the scale factor is 1. |
| Do the figures face different directions? | They may still be congruent. |
| Do the figures have equal area only? | Equal area alone is not enough. |

## Memory Anchors

Congruent means same size and same shape.

Different location is okay. Different orientation is okay. Different side lengths or angle measures are not okay.

Rigid transformations preserve congruence:

- slide: translation
- flip: reflection
- turn: rotation

## Common Traps

- Do not reject figures just because their coordinates are different.
- Do not reject figures just because one is rotated or reflected.
- Do not accept figures just because they look similar.
- Do not use equal area alone as proof of congruence.
- Do not confuse similar with congruent. Similar figures can be scaled; congruent figures cannot change size.

## Pause And Think

A rectangle is 6 units by 4 units. Another rectangle is 4 units by 6 units.

These rectangles are congruent. Turning a 6-by-4 rectangle can make it appear as a 4-by-6 rectangle, but the side lengths still match.

Now compare a 6-by-4 rectangle with a 12-by-8 rectangle. The second rectangle is a scaled copy, so it is similar but not congruent.

## Apply It

Suppose Figure A is reflected across the y-axis and then translated 3 units right. Figure B is the result.

A reflection is rigid. A translation is rigid. A sequence of rigid transformations is still rigid.

So Figure A and Figure B are congruent. Their coordinates and orientation may be different, but corresponding side lengths and angles are preserved.
