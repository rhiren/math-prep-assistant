# Operations with Scientific Notation

## The big idea

Scientific notation makes multiplication and division efficient because powers
of ten follow exponent rules.

For addition and subtraction, the powers of ten must match first.

## Multiplication

Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents.

`(3 × 10⁴)(2 × 10⁵)`

`= (3 × 2) × 10⁴⁺⁵`

`= 6 × 10⁹`

If the coefficient is not between 1 and 10, normalize the answer.

`(7 × 10³)(4 × 10²) = 28 × 10⁵ = 2.8 × 10⁶`

## Division

Divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents.

`(8 × 10⁶) ÷ (2 × 10²)`

`= (8 ÷ 2) × 10⁶⁻²`

`= 4 × 10⁴`

Normalize if needed:

`(3 × 10⁵) ÷ (6 × 10²) = 0.5 × 10³ = 5 × 10²`

## Addition and subtraction

Add or subtract only after the powers of ten match.

`3 × 10⁵ + 4 × 10⁴`

Rewrite `4 × 10⁴` as `0.4 × 10⁵`.

`3 × 10⁵ + 0.4 × 10⁵ = 3.4 × 10⁵`

For subtraction:

`6 × 10³ - 2 × 10² = 6 × 10³ - 0.2 × 10³ = 5.8 × 10³`

## Estimation

Scientific notation is useful for estimating with extreme quantities.

`(6.1 × 10⁷)(1.9 × 10³)`

is about:

`(6 × 2) × 10¹⁰ = 12 × 10¹⁰ = 1.2 × 10¹¹`

## Strategy

1. Identify the operation.
2. Multiply/divide coefficients and add/subtract exponents for products and quotients.
3. Match powers of ten before adding or subtracting.
4. Normalize the final coefficient so it is at least 1 and less than 10.
