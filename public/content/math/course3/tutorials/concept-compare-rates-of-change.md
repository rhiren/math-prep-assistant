# Compare Rates of Change

## Big Idea

Rates of change can appear in different forms: a table, graph, equation, pair
of points, or story. To compare them fairly, turn each one into a slope or unit
rate.

The larger rate of change is the relationship where `y` changes faster for
each 1-unit change in `x`.

## What To Notice

- In `y = mx + b`, compare the `m` values.
- From a table, compare `change in y / change in x`.
- From a graph, compare steepness using two clear points.
- In a context, divide output change by input change.
- Negative rates need careful comparison: `-1` is greater than `-4`, but `-4`
  decreases faster.

## Worked Example

Runner A travels `15` miles in `3` hours.

`15 / 3 = 5` miles per hour.

Runner B follows `y = 4x`, where `y` is miles and `x` is hours.

Runner B's rate is `4` miles per hour.

Runner A has the greater rate of change because `5 > 4`.

## Mixed Representation Example

A table shows points `(2, 10)` and `(6, 22)`.

- change in `y`: `22 - 10 = 12`
- change in `x`: `6 - 2 = 4`
- rate: `12 / 4 = 3`

Compare this with `y = 2x + 8`: the equation's rate is `2`, so the table has
the greater rate of change.

## Common Traps

- Do not compare y-intercepts when the question asks for rate of change.
- Do not compare total amounts from different input values.
- Watch units: dollars per ticket and tickets per dollar are not the same.

## Quick Self-Check

Before choosing, make sure every representation has been converted into the
same kind of number: change in `y` per 1 change in `x`.
