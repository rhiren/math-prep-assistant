# Unit 1 Mixed Review

## Big Idea

Unit 1 connects two big geometry ideas.

First, rigid transformations move figures without changing their size or shape. Translations slide, reflections flip, and rotations turn. If one figure can be mapped onto another using rigid transformations, the figures are congruent.

Second, angle relationships let you reason without measuring. Parallel lines, triangle angle sums, and exterior angles all give dependable equations for missing angles.

The mixed review goal is to choose the right relationship before doing the calculation.

## What To Notice

- Translations add the same horizontal and vertical change to every point.
- Reflections change signs in predictable ways across the x-axis, y-axis, or origin.
- Rotations about the origin follow coordinate rules.
- Rigid transformations preserve side lengths, angle measures, perimeter, and area.
- Congruent figures have matching corresponding sides and angles.
- Parallel-line angle pairs can be equal or supplementary.
- Triangle interior angles add to 180 degrees.
- A triangle exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles.

## Worked Example

Point A(2, -5) is reflected across the x-axis and then translated 3 units left and 4 units up.

Reflect across the x-axis:

A(2, -5) becomes (2, 5)

Translate 3 left and 4 up:

(2 - 3, 5 + 4) = (-1, 9)

So the final image is A'(-1, 9).

Now suppose a triangle has remote interior angles of 43 degrees and 72 degrees. The exterior angle is:

43 + 72 = 115

The adjacent interior angle is:

180 - 115 = 65

## Strategy

| Problem type | First question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Coordinate transformation | Which rule applies first? |
| Transformation sequence | What is the intermediate image? |
| Congruence claim | Were only rigid transformations used? |
| Parallel lines | Is the angle pair equal or supplementary? |
| Triangle angle sum | Do the three interior angles add to 180? |
| Exterior angle | Which two angles are remote interior angles? |

## Memory Anchors

Rigid transformations preserve size and shape:

- translation: slide
- reflection: flip
- rotation: turn

Angle relationships:

- corresponding angles are equal
- alternate interior and alternate exterior angles are equal
- same-side interior angles add to 180
- triangle interior angles add to 180
- exterior angle = remote interior + remote interior

## Common Traps

- Do not apply transformations out of order.
- Do not mix up clockwise and counterclockwise rotation rules.
- Do not call figures congruent after a dilation unless the scale factor is 1.
- Do not assume every angle pair near parallel lines is equal.
- Do not subtract from 90 unless the problem gives a right triangle.
- Do not use the adjacent interior angle as a remote interior angle.

## Pause And Think

A student says, "The image changed position, so it cannot be congruent."

That reasoning misses the key idea. A figure can move and still be congruent.

The better question is: did the transformation preserve all side lengths and angle measures?

If the movement was a translation, reflection, rotation, or sequence of those, then the image is congruent to the original.

## Apply It

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. Same-side interior angles are labeled 5x degrees and 7x degrees.

Same-side interior angles are supplementary:

5x + 7x = 180

12x = 180

x = 15

The angles are 75 degrees and 105 degrees.

Check: 75 + 105 = 180.
