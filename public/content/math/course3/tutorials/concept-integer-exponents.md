# Integer Exponents

## The big idea

An exponent is a compact way to show repeated multiplication.

`5³ = 5 × 5 × 5 = 125`

In `5³`:

- `5` is the **base**
- `3` is the **exponent**
- `5³` is a **power**

The exponent tells how many times the base appears as a factor. It does not
mean “multiply the base by the exponent.”

`5³` is `5 × 5 × 5`, not `5 × 3`.

## Read and write powers

| Power | Repeated multiplication | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `2⁴` | `2 × 2 × 2 × 2` | `16` |
| `3³` | `3 × 3 × 3` | `27` |
| `10⁵` | `10 × 10 × 10 × 10 × 10` | `100,000` |
| `x⁴` | `x × x × x × x` | depends on `x` |

An exponent of `1` means the base appears once:

`a¹ = a`

This lesson uses positive integer exponents. Zero and negative exponents come
in the next concept.

## Signs and parentheses

Parentheses determine whether a negative sign is part of the base.

`(-2)⁴ = (-2)(-2)(-2)(-2) = 16`

`-2⁴ = -(2⁴) = -16`

For a negative base:

- an even exponent gives a positive result
- an odd exponent gives a negative result

Examples:

- `(-3)² = 9`
- `(-3)³ = -27`

## Product of powers

When multiplying powers with the **same base**, add the exponents.

`aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ`

Example:

`x³ × x⁴`

`= (x × x × x)(x × x × x × x)`

`= x⁷`

There are seven factors of `x`, so:

`x³ × x⁴ = x³⁺⁴ = x⁷`

Only combine exponents this way when the bases match.

`2³ × 5³` does not use the same-base rule. Because the exponents match, it can
instead be written as:

`2³ × 5³ = (2 × 5)³ = 10³`

## Quotient of powers

When dividing powers with the **same nonzero base**, subtract the exponents.

`aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ`

For this lesson, the numerator exponent will be greater than the denominator
exponent.

Example:

`y⁷ ÷ y³`

Three factors of `y` cancel:

`(y × y × y × y × y × y × y) / (y × y × y)`

Four factors remain:

`y⁷ ÷ y³ = y⁷⁻³ = y⁴`

The base must be nonzero because division by zero is undefined.

## Power of a power

When raising a power to another power, multiply the exponents.

`(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ`

Example:

`(x³)⁴`

This means four groups of `x³`:

`x³ × x³ × x³ × x³ = x¹²`

Therefore:

`(x³)⁴ = x³×⁴ = x¹²`

Do not add the exponents in a power of a power.

## Power of a product

An exponent outside parentheses applies to every factor inside.

`(ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ`

Example:

`(3xy)²`

`= (3xy)(3xy)`

`= 9x²y²`

The coefficient is raised to the power too.

`(2x)³ = 2³x³ = 8x³`

## Power of a quotient

An exponent applies to both the numerator and denominator.

`(a/b)ⁿ = aⁿ/bⁿ`

Example:

`(x/3)² = x²/9`

because:

`(x/3)(x/3) = x²/9`

The denominator must be nonzero.

## Combine coefficients and powers

Treat numerical coefficients and variable powers separately.

`4x² × 3x⁵`

Multiply the coefficients:

`4 × 3 = 12`

Add the exponents on the common base `x`:

`x² × x⁵ = x⁷`

Therefore:

`4x² × 3x⁵ = 12x⁷`

For division:

`12a⁶ ÷ 3a² = 4a⁴`

Divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents.

## Several variables

Apply exponent rules to each base separately.

`(a²b³)²`

`= (a²)²(b³)²`

`= a⁴b⁶`

For a product:

`a³b² × a⁴b`

`= a³⁺⁴b²⁺¹`

`= a⁷b³`

Do not combine the exponents of different bases.

## Geometry connections

Exponents naturally describe area and volume.

If a square has side length `3x²`, its area is:

`(3x²)² = 9x⁴`

If a cube has edge length `2a²`, its volume is:

`(2a²)³ = 8a⁶`

The outside exponent applies to both the coefficient and the variable power.

## A reliable strategy

1. Identify the operation: multiplication, division, or a power.
2. Check which bases match.
3. Apply the correct exponent rule.
4. Handle numerical coefficients separately.
5. Apply outside exponents to every factor inside parentheses.
6. Check signs and parentheses.
7. Test with a small numerical value when you want to verify an identity.

## Common mistakes

- Multiplying the base and exponent: `4³` is not `12`.
- Multiplying exponents in a product: `x² × x³ = x⁵`, not `x⁶`.
- Adding exponents in a power of a power: `(x²)³ = x⁶`, not `x⁵`.
- Applying a power only to one factor: `(2x)³ = 8x³`, not `2x³`.
- Combining unlike bases: `x²y³` cannot be rewritten as one power of `x` or
  one power of `y`.
- Losing the sign: `(-2)⁴ = 16`, while `-2⁴ = -16`.
- Using the product rule for addition: `x³ + x³ = 2x³`, not `x⁶`.

## Quick practice

1. Evaluate `3⁴`.
2. Simplify `x⁵ × x³`.
3. Simplify `a⁹ ÷ a⁴`.
4. Simplify `(m³)⁵`.
5. Simplify `(2xy²)³`.

### Answers

1. `81`
2. `x⁸`
3. `a⁵`
4. `m¹⁵`
5. `8x³y⁶`
