# Powers of Ten

## The big idea

Powers of ten describe place value. They make very large and very small numbers
easier to read, compare, and compute with.

`10⁴ = 10,000`

`10⁻⁴ = 0.0001`

Positive powers move right to larger place values. Negative powers move left to
smaller place values.

## Positive powers of ten

For positive exponents, `10ⁿ` is 1 followed by `n` zeros.

| Power | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `10¹` | `10` |
| `10²` | `100` |
| `10³` | `1,000` |
| `10⁶` | `1,000,000` |

## Negative powers of ten

Negative powers of ten are reciprocals.

`10⁻¹ = 1/10 = 0.1`

`10⁻³ = 1/1,000 = 0.001`

Each step down divides by 10.

## Moving decimals

Multiplying by `10³` moves the decimal 3 places right.

`4.7 × 10³ = 4,700`

Multiplying by `10⁻³` moves the decimal 3 places left.

`4.7 × 10⁻³ = 0.0047`

## Comparing powers of ten

The exponent tells the size.

`10⁵ > 10²`

`10⁻² > 10⁻⁵`

For negative powers, the exponent closer to zero is larger because `0.01` is
larger than `0.00001`.

## Exponent rules still work

`10³ × 10⁴ = 10⁷`

`10³ ÷ 10⁸ = 10⁻⁵`

`(10⁻²)³ = 10⁻⁶`

These rules prepare you for scientific notation.
