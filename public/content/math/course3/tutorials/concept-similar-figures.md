# Similar Figures

## Big Idea

Similar figures have the same shape, but they do not have to be the same size.

For two figures to be similar, two things must stay organized:

- corresponding angles have equal measures
- corresponding side lengths are proportional

That means one scale factor should connect every matching side length from one figure to the other.

## What To Notice

- Corresponding parts are matching parts in the same position.
- If two figures are named in order, such as `ABCD ~ WXYZ`, then `A` matches `W`, `B` matches `X`, `C` matches `Y`, and `D` matches `Z`.
- Equal angles alone can show triangles are similar, but for general polygons you also need proportional corresponding sides.
- Congruent figures are similar with scale factor `1`.
- Similar figures can be enlargements or reductions.

## Worked Example

Triangle `ABC` has side lengths `4, 6, 8`.

Triangle `DEF` has corresponding side lengths `10, 15, 20`.

Compare image side divided by original side:

`10 / 4 = 2.5`

`15 / 6 = 2.5`

`20 / 8 = 2.5`

The ratios match, so the side lengths are proportional. The scale factor from `ABC` to `DEF` is `2.5`.

If the corresponding angles also match, the triangles are similar.

## Missing Side Strategy

Suppose two figures are similar. One side changes from `5` units to `20` units.

The scale factor is:

`20 / 5 = 4`

If another original side is `7` units, its image side is:

`7 * 4 = 28`

The missing side is `28` units.

## Common Traps

- Do not compare sides that are not corresponding.
- Do not subtract side lengths to find scale factor. Divide image by original.
- Do not assume figures are similar just because they look alike.
- Do not require similar figures to be the same size. That would be congruence.
- Do not use area scale factor for side lengths. Side lengths use `k`; areas use `k^2`.

## Angle Correspondence

The order of the letters matters.

If `ABCD ~ WXYZ`, then angle `B` corresponds to angle `X`.

So if angle `B` is `72` degrees, angle `X` is also `72` degrees.

Side `AB` corresponds to side `WX`, side `BC` corresponds to side `XY`, and so on.

## Perimeter And Area

If similar figures have scale factor `k`:

| Measure | Change |
| --- | --- |
| side length | multiply by `k` |
| perimeter | multiply by `k` |
| area | multiply by `k^2` |

So if the scale factor is `3`, the perimeter is `3` times as large, but the area is `9` times as large.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 things to check:

- matching angles
- matching side ratios
- correct correspondence order

2 scale facts:

- lengths and perimeter use `k`
- area uses `k^2`

1 question to ask:

Am I comparing the right matching parts?
