# Sequences of Rigid Transformations

## Big Idea

A sequence of transformations means doing more than one move, one after another.

In this lesson, the moves are rigid transformations: translations, reflections, and rotations. Each move preserves distance and angle measure. Because every step is rigid, the final image is still congruent to the original figure.

The most important habit is to apply the steps in order. The first step creates an intermediate image, and the next step starts from that intermediate image.

## What To Notice

- A point labeled P is the original point.
- P' is the image after the first transformation.
- P'' is the image after the second transformation.
- The order of transformations can change the final image.
- Translations, reflections, and rotations preserve side lengths and angle measures.
- To work backward, undo the steps in reverse order.

## Worked Example

Point A is (2, 3). Translate A by <4, -1>, then reflect the result across the x-axis.

First apply the translation:

(2, 3) -> (6, 2)

So A' is (6, 2).

Now reflect A' across the x-axis. The x-coordinate stays the same and the y-coordinate changes sign:

(6, 2) -> (6, -2)

So A'' is (6, -2).

## Strategy

| Task | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Follow a sequence | Apply the first step, write the intermediate image, then apply the next step. |
| Compare two sequences | Test each sequence on the same starting point. |
| Find a preimage | Undo the final step first, then keep working backward. |
| Prove congruence | Show one figure maps onto the other using only rigid transformations. |
| Check your work | Side lengths and angle measures should stay the same. |

## Memory Anchors

Think of a sequence like directions on a map. If you do the directions out of order, you may not end in the same place.

Prime notation tracks progress:

- P: original point
- P': after one transformation
- P'': after two transformations

A sequence made only of slides, flips, and turns keeps the figure congruent.

## Common Traps

- Do not apply every transformation to the original point. Each step starts from the result of the step before it.
- Do not switch the order unless the problem says the order can change.
- Do not treat a sequence with a dilation as rigid. Dilations can change size.
- Do not forget to transform every vertex of a figure.
- Do not assume orientation stays the same. Reflections and rotations can change the direction a figure faces.

## Pause And Think

Point B is (-3, 4). Reflect B across the y-axis, then translate by <2, -5>.

Reflect across the y-axis:

(-3, 4) -> (3, 4)

Translate by <2, -5>:

(3, 4) -> (5, -1)

So B'' is (5, -1).

If you translated first and reflected second, you would not necessarily land at the same final point. That is why order matters.

## Apply It

Triangle PQR has vertices P(1, 2), Q(4, 2), and R(2, 5). Translate by <-3, 1>, then rotate 180 degrees about the origin.

After the translation:

- P(1, 2) -> P'(-2, 3)
- Q(4, 2) -> Q'(1, 3)
- R(2, 5) -> R'(-1, 6)

After the 180 degree rotation:

- P'(-2, 3) -> P''(2, -3)
- Q'(1, 3) -> Q''(-1, -3)
- R'(-1, 6) -> R''(1, -6)

The final triangle is congruent to the original because both steps were rigid transformations.
