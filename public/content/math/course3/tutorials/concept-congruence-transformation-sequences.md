# Congruence from Transformation Sequences

## Big Idea

Two figures are congruent when one figure can be moved exactly onto the other using rigid transformations.

A rigid transformation is a move that does not stretch, shrink, bend, or distort a figure. Translations, reflections, and rotations are rigid. A sequence made only from those moves is still rigid, so it preserves side lengths and angle measures.

This lesson is about using a transformation sequence as evidence. Instead of only saying two shapes look the same, you learn to say why they must be congruent.

## What To Notice

- A translation slides every point the same distance and direction.
- A reflection flips a figure across a line.
- A rotation turns a figure around a center.
- Any sequence of translations, reflections, and rotations preserves distance and angle measure.
- If a sequence includes a dilation with scale factor other than 1, the sequence is not rigid.
- A congruence statement must match corresponding vertices in the order created by the transformation.

## Worked Example

Triangle ABC is reflected across the y-axis and then translated 4 units right to make triangle DEF.

Both steps are rigid transformations:

- A reflection preserves side lengths and angle measures.
- A translation also preserves side lengths and angle measures.

Because every step is rigid, triangle ABC is congruent to triangle DEF.

That means corresponding parts match:

- side AB matches side DE
- side BC matches side EF
- side AC matches side DF
- angle A matches angle D
- angle B matches angle E
- angle C matches angle F

## Strategy

| Task | How to reason |
| --- | --- |
| Decide if two figures are congruent | Look for a sequence using only translations, reflections, and rotations. |
| Explain why congruent | Name the rigid transformations and say they preserve side lengths and angles. |
| Reject a sequence | Check whether it includes a dilation, stretch, or changed measurement. |
| Match corresponding parts | Follow the vertex order from preimage to image. |
| Work backward | Undo the steps in reverse order, but the relationship is still congruence if all steps are rigid. |

## Memory Anchors

Rigid means no size or shape change.

Congruence proof sentence:

The figures are congruent because a sequence of rigid transformations maps one figure onto the other.

Rigid moves preserve:

- lengths
- angle measures
- perimeter
- area

Rigid moves may change:

- location
- orientation
- coordinates
- vertex order if a reflection reverses orientation

## Common Traps

- Do not require figures to face the same direction.
- Do not reject a figure because it moved to a different quadrant.
- Do not accept a dilation as a congruence move unless the scale factor is exactly 1.
- Do not use equal area alone as proof of congruence.
- Do not mix up corresponding vertices after a reflection or rotation.
- Do not say a sequence is rigid just because the final figure looks similar.

## Pause And Think

Suppose quadrilateral WXYZ is rotated 90 degrees and then translated left 3 units to make quadrilateral ABCD.

The final figure may be in a different place and may face a different direction. That is okay. Rotation and translation are both rigid, so every side length and every angle measure is preserved.

The correct conclusion is:

Quadrilateral WXYZ is congruent to quadrilateral ABCD.

## Apply It

A student says, "The figures are congruent because they both have area 24 square units."

That is not enough evidence. Two rectangles can both have area 24, such as a 3-by-8 rectangle and a 4-by-6 rectangle, but they are not congruent because their side lengths do not match.

A stronger explanation is:

The figures are congruent because a reflection followed by a translation maps the first figure exactly onto the second figure. Since both transformations are rigid, corresponding side lengths and angles are preserved.

Can I describe a rigid transformation sequence that maps one figure exactly onto the other?
