# Homeostasis and Feedback in Body Systems

## Hook

During exercise, breathing and heart rate rise. During rest, they move back toward resting levels. The body is not random; it is constantly adjusting conditions so cells can keep working.

## Big Idea

Homeostasis means keeping internal conditions within a workable range. Body systems use receptors, signals, control centers, and responses to adjust to change. Memory anchor: **Detect, signal, respond, rebalance.**

## Learn It

- A stimulus is a change the body can detect.
- Receptors detect information such as light, temperature, or chemical levels.
- Signals carry information to control centers such as the brain.
- Responses help restore conditions that cells need.

## Build The Model

Use this pathway:

`stimulus -> receptor -> signal -> control center -> response -> condition shifts toward balance`

Example:

`exercise -> carbon dioxide rises -> receptors and brain respond -> breathing increases -> gas levels move toward balance`

## Memory Anchors

- **Detect, signal, respond, rebalance.**
- **Cells need workable conditions.**
- **Systems adjust together.**
- **Feedback is not random.**

## Evidence Rules

| Evidence | What It Helps Explain |
|---|---|
| heart rate rises during exercise | cells need faster material delivery |
| pupils shrink in bright light | sensory detection led to a response |
| body temperature returns toward normal | feedback helped restore balance |
| thirst after water loss | response can help water balance |

## Common Traps

- Trap: "Fast responses do not use the nervous system."  
  Fix: Some nervous-system pathways are very fast.
- Trap: "Homeostasis means nothing changes."  
  Fix: It means conditions stay within workable ranges.
- Trap: "One system works alone."  
  Fix: Responses usually involve interacting systems.

## Pause And Think

## Apply It

If body temperature rises, sweating and changes in blood flow can help cool the body. A strong model shows the stimulus, detection, response, and movement back toward balance.

## Try At Home

Hold an ice-cold cup wrapped in a towel for 20 seconds, then notice what your hand senses after you let go. Write the pathway: stimulus, receptor, signal, response. Keep the cold brief and comfortable.

- - Body systems interact to support cells.

- - Do not say responses happen before detection.
- - Do not confuse balance with no change.
