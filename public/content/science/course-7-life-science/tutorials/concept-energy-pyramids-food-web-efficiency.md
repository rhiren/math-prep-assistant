# Energy Pyramids and Food Web Efficiency

## Hook

A field may contain many grass plants, fewer grasshoppers, fewer frogs, and only a few hawks. The pattern is not random. Energy becomes less available at each feeding level.

Food webs show who eats whom. Energy pyramids show why the numbers often shrink.

## Big Idea

Energy flows through ecosystems, but only some energy stored in one level becomes new biomass in the next level. Much energy is used by organisms or released as heat.

Memory anchor: **Energy thins as it climbs.**

## Learn It

Producers capture energy, usually from sunlight. Consumers get energy by eating other organisms.

At each transfer:

- organisms use energy to live
- some energy leaves as heat
- only some energy becomes new body matter for the next consumer
- higher levels usually have less available energy

Matter cycles, but energy flows in one main direction and becomes less available to living things.

## Build The Model

Use this chain:

`producer energy -> primary consumer -> secondary consumer -> less available energy each step`

Example:

`grass -> grasshopper -> frog -> hawk`

## Memory Anchors

- **Energy thins as it climbs.**
- **Producers start the pyramid.**
- **Matter cycles; energy flows.**
- **Heat explains missing usable energy.**

## Evidence Rules

Strong energy-pyramid explanations use:

- feeding-level position
- relative energy or biomass amounts
- arrows showing energy transfer
- a reason energy decreases at each step

## Common Traps

- Trap: "All energy passes to the next consumer."  
  Fix: Much energy is used or released as heat.
- Trap: "Arrows show what an organism wants."  
  Fix: Arrows show energy or matter transfer.
- Trap: "Matter and energy behave the same way."  
  Fix: Matter cycles; energy flows and becomes less available.

## Pause And Think

- Why are producers at the base?
- Why is less energy available to hawks than to grasshoppers?
- What is the difference between matter cycling and energy flowing?

## Apply It

A pond has algae, small water insects, fish, and herons. If algae decline, the pyramid base shrinks, so less energy is available to higher levels.

## Try At Home

Build a paper energy pyramid from a meal. Put the plant source at the base, then add any consumer levels involved. Use smaller paper pieces higher up to represent less available energy.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 things to remember:

- Producers form the energy base.
- Energy decreases at higher feeding levels.
- Food-web arrows show transfer, not preference.

2 traps to avoid:

- Do not say all energy moves to the next level.
- Do not confuse matter cycling with energy flow.

1 explain-it prompt:

- Why can one field support more grass than hawks?
