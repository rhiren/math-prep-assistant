# Body Systems and Information Processing

## Hook

You touch something hot and pull your hand away almost instantly. Your skin, nerves, muscles, and brain all help. No single part does the whole job alone.

## Big Idea

Multicellular organisms are organized in levels, and body systems interact to keep the organism alive.

Memory anchor: **Systems work together.**

## Learn It

Organization in multicellular organisms:

`cells -> tissues -> organs -> organ systems -> organism`

Examples:

- Digestive system breaks food into useful materials.
- Respiratory system brings in oxygen.
- Circulatory system moves materials around the body.
- Muscular and skeletal systems support movement.
- Nervous system receives information and helps coordinate responses.

Sensory receptors detect information such as light, sound, pressure, temperature, or chemicals. Signals travel through the nervous system and can lead to a response.

## Build The Model

Use this chain for information processing:

`stimulus -> receptor -> nerve signal -> brain/spinal cord -> response`

Example:

`hot surface -> skin receptor -> nerve signal -> spinal cord/brain -> hand pulls away`

## Memory Anchors

- **Cells build up.** Cells form tissues, tissues form organs, organs form systems.
- **Systems interact.** One body job often needs several systems.
- **Receptors detect.** They sense information before the body responds.

## Compare And Contrast

| Level | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| cell | smallest living unit | muscle cell |
| tissue | similar cells working together | muscle tissue |
| organ | tissues working together | heart |
| organ system | organs working together | circulatory system |

## Common Traps

- Trap: "One system does the whole job."  
  Fix: Most body functions require several systems interacting.
- Trap: "All receptors detect the same thing."  
  Fix: Different receptors detect different kinds of information.
- Trap: "The brain acts before information is detected."  
  Fix: A stimulus must be detected and signaled first.

## Pause And Think

Answer these without looking back:

- What comes between cells and organs?
- Why does sprinting require more than the muscular system?
- What is the first step in an information-processing chain?

## Apply It

A student runs across a field. Her breathing speeds up, her heart beats faster, and her leg muscles move.

This shows system interaction: respiratory brings in oxygen, circulatory moves oxygen and food molecules, and muscles use energy to move.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 things to remember:

- Cells, tissues, organs, and systems are levels of organization.
- Body systems interact.
- Receptors help detect information.

2 traps to avoid:

- Do not explain a body function with only one system if several are involved.
- Do not skip the receptor step in sensory responses.

1 explain-it prompt:

- How do body systems work together when someone exercises?
