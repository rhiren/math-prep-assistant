# Digestion, Molecules, and Cell Fuel

## Hook

A cracker starts crunchy, then becomes softer and sweeter as you chew. The food did not disappear. It began breaking into smaller molecules that cells can use.

Your body is a transport-and-processing system, not just a food container.

## Big Idea

Digestion breaks large food molecules into smaller molecules. The circulatory system transports those molecules, and cells use some of them in respiration to release usable energy.

Memory anchor: **Food becomes cell materials.**

## Learn It

Body systems work together:

- digestion breaks food into smaller molecules
- absorption moves useful molecules into the blood
- circulation transports molecules to cells
- cells use glucose and oxygen in cellular respiration

Food gives matter for growth and repair, and it gives molecules that cells can use to release energy. Energy itself is not a material chunk that gets stored in a food label; cells transform energy through chemical processes.

## Build The Model

Use this chain:

`food molecule -> digestion -> absorbed small molecules -> blood transport -> cell respiration or building`

Example:

`starch -> glucose -> blood -> muscle cell -> cellular respiration`

## Memory Anchors

- **Break down before build up.**
- **Blood is a delivery system.**
- **Cells use molecules, not whole sandwiches.**
- **Respiration happens in cells.**

## Evidence Rules

Strong explanations connect systems:

- digestive evidence, such as food breaking down
- transport evidence, such as molecules in blood
- cell evidence, such as glucose and oxygen being used
- cause/effect evidence, such as more activity increasing cell demand

## Common Traps

- Trap: "The stomach gives energy directly to muscles."  
  Fix: Digestion breaks food down; cells release usable energy from molecules.
- Trap: "Food only gives energy."  
  Fix: Food also provides matter for growth and repair.
- Trap: "One body system works alone."  
  Fix: Digestion, circulation, respiration, and cells interact.

## Pause And Think

- Why must large food molecules be broken down?
- How does blood connect digestion to cells?
- Why do active muscle cells need more oxygen and glucose?

## Apply It

A runner eats pasta before a race. A strong model does not stop at "pasta gives energy." It traces starch to glucose, glucose transport in blood, and glucose use in muscle cells.

## Try At Home

Chew an unsalted cracker slowly for 30 seconds and notice whether the taste changes. Write a possible explanation: large starch molecules begin breaking into smaller sugar molecules. Stop if any food ingredient is not safe for you.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 things to remember:

- Digestion breaks food into smaller molecules.
- Blood transports useful molecules to cells.
- Cells use molecules in respiration and building.

2 traps to avoid:

- Do not say whole food travels unchanged to muscles.
- Do not say one system does the whole job alone.

1 explain-it prompt:

- How does a bite of food become useful to a muscle cell?
