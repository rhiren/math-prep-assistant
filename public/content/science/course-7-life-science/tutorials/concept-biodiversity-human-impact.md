# Biodiversity and Human Impact

## Hook

Two gardens face the same disease. One garden has only one kind of plant. The other has many kinds of plants. The disease spreads quickly through the one-plant garden, but the mixed garden keeps more plants alive.

Variety can make a system more resilient.

## Big Idea

Biodiversity is the variety of life in an ecosystem. Human actions can reduce or protect biodiversity, and good solutions should be supported by evidence.

Memory anchor: **More variety can mean more resilience.**

## Learn It

Biodiversity includes:

- different species
- genetic variation within a species
- different roles organisms play in ecosystems

Ecosystems provide services such as clean water, soil formation, pollination, food, and materials.

Human activities can change habitats, resources, and populations. Impacts may include pollution, land development, invasive species, overharvesting, or climate-related changes.

## Build The Model

Use this solution chain:

`human action -> ecosystem change -> affected organisms -> evidence-based solution -> tradeoffs`

Example:

`new road -> habitat split -> animals cross road unsafely -> wildlife crossing -> cost and location tradeoffs`

## Memory Anchors

- **Biodiversity means variety of life.**
- **Ecosystem services help people and organisms.**
- **Solutions need evidence.**
- **Tradeoffs matter.**

## Evidence Rules

A strong design solution should:

- address the cause of the problem
- use evidence
- consider tradeoffs
- be tested and improved

## Common Traps

- Trap: "Any action that sounds helpful is a good solution."  
  Fix: A good solution targets the cause and uses evidence.
- Trap: "Biodiversity only means number of species."  
  Fix: It also includes genetic variation and ecosystem roles.
- Trap: "Human impact is always one simple effect."  
  Fix: Effects can ripple through habitats, resources, and populations.

## Pause And Think

Answer these without looking back:

- What does biodiversity mean?
- Why can variety help an ecosystem respond to change?
- What makes a solution evidence-based?

## Apply It

A wetland is losing frogs after nearby runoff increases algae growth and lowers oxygen in the water.

A strong solution should target runoff or water quality, not just move frogs away without fixing the cause.

## Try At Home

Choose a window view, yard, or sidewalk strip and list as many different living things or living-thing clues as you can. Repeat on another day and compare variety, not just total count.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 things to remember:

- Biodiversity is variety of life.
- Human actions can change ecosystems.
- Good solutions use evidence and consider tradeoffs.

2 traps to avoid:

- Do not count only species and ignore genetic or role diversity.
- Do not choose a solution that misses the cause.

1 explain-it prompt:

- How can biodiversity help an ecosystem recover from a disturbance?
