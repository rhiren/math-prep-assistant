# Solve Multi-Step Equations

## The big idea

A multi-step equation needs more than one inverse operation to isolate the
variable.

Example:

`3x + 5 = 20`

First undo the `+ 5`:

`3x = 15`

Then undo multiplication by 3:

`x = 5`

The goal is to keep the equation balanced while making the variable stand
alone.

## Keep the balance

An equation is like a balance. Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the
other side.

`4x - 7 = 21`

Add 7 to both sides:

`4x = 28`

Divide both sides by 4:

`x = 7`

## Undo in reverse order

Think about the expression attached to the variable.

In `5x + 2 = 32`, the variable is:

1. multiplied by 5
2. then increased by 2

To solve, undo those steps in reverse:

1. subtract 2
2. divide by 5

`5x + 2 = 32`

`5x = 30`

`x = 6`

## Combine like terms first

Sometimes the variable side has like terms.

`3x + 2x + 4 = 29`

Combine `3x + 2x` first:

`5x + 4 = 29`

Then solve:

`5x = 25`

`x = 5`

## Watch negative signs

Negative coefficients are common in multi-step equations.

`-3x + 8 = 20`

Subtract 8 from both sides:

`-3x = 12`

Divide by `-3`:

`x = -4`

A negative coefficient means the final division may change the sign.

## Fractions and decimals

Fractions and decimals use the same balance idea.

`x/4 + 3 = 8`

Subtract 3:

`x/4 = 5`

Multiply by 4:

`x = 20`

For decimals:

`0.5x - 2 = 6`

Add 2:

`0.5x = 8`

Divide by `0.5`:

`x = 16`

## Check your answer

After solving, substitute the answer into the original equation.

For `2x + 9 = 25`, if `x = 8`:

`2(8) + 9 = 16 + 9 = 25`

The check works, so `x = 8`.

## A reliable strategy

1. Simplify each side if needed.
2. Undo addition or subtraction.
3. Undo multiplication or division.
4. Track negative signs carefully.
5. Substitute the answer back into the original equation.
