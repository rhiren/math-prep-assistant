# Triangle Angle Relationships

## Big Idea

Every triangle has three interior angles, and those three angles always add to 180 degrees.

That one fact lets you find missing angles, decide whether a set of angle measures can make a triangle, and reason about special triangles such as right, isosceles, and equilateral triangles.

This lesson is not just about subtracting from 180. The important move is deciding which relationship fits the triangle before doing the arithmetic.

## What To Notice

- The three interior angles of a triangle add to 180 degrees.
- In a right triangle, the two acute angles add to 90 degrees.
- In an isosceles triangle, the angles opposite the equal sides are equal.
- In an equilateral triangle, all three angles are 60 degrees.
- A triangle is acute if all three angles are less than 90 degrees.
- A triangle is right if one angle is exactly 90 degrees.
- A triangle is obtuse if one angle is greater than 90 degrees.
- A set of angle measures cannot form a triangle unless the sum is exactly 180 degrees.

## Worked Example

A triangle has angle measures of 38 degrees, 74 degrees, and x degrees.

Use the triangle angle sum:

38 + 74 + x = 180

112 + x = 180

x = 68

The missing angle is 68 degrees.

Now classify the triangle. Since all three angles are less than 90 degrees, the triangle is acute.

## Strategy

| Situation | Relationship to use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two angles are known | Subtract their sum from 180 | 180 - 42 - 71 |
| A right triangle gives one acute angle | Subtract from 90 | 90 - 34 |
| Two triangle angles are equal | Use the same variable for both | x + x + 80 = 180 |
| Angles are written with expressions | Add all three expressions to 180 | 2x + (x + 10) + 50 = 180 |
| Angles are in a ratio | Add the ratio parts first | 2:3:4 has 9 parts |
| A set of angles is proposed | Check the total | 45 + 55 + 80 = 180 |

## Memory Anchors

Think of a triangle as holding exactly 180 degrees of angle space.

If one angle gets larger, the other two must share less space.

For special triangles:

- right triangle: the two non-right angles share 90 degrees
- isosceles triangle: two angles match
- equilateral triangle: three angles match, so each is 60 degrees

## Common Traps

- Do not subtract from 90 unless the triangle is right and you are finding the other acute angle.
- Do not assume two angles are equal just because a drawing looks symmetric.
- Do not accept angle sets that add to 179 or 181 degrees.
- Do not forget to include all three angle expressions in an equation.
- Do not classify a triangle before finding the missing angle.
- Do not confuse equal sides with equal angles: equal sides have equal opposite angles.

## Pause And Think

A student says, "The triangle has angles 40 degrees, 60 degrees, and 90 degrees because those all look reasonable."

The measures are individually reasonable, but together they add to:

40 + 60 + 90 = 190

That cannot be a triangle. A valid triangle must have interior angles that add to exactly 180 degrees.

## Apply It

Suppose an isosceles triangle has a vertex angle of 52 degrees.

The two base angles are equal, so first find how much angle measure remains:

180 - 52 = 128

Then divide it equally:

128 / 2 = 64

The base angles are 64 degrees and 64 degrees.

Check: 52 + 64 + 64 = 180.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 facts:

- triangle angles add to 180 degrees
- right triangle acute angles add to 90 degrees
- isosceles base angles are equal

2 questions to ask:

- Do I know two angles, or do I need an equation?
- Is this a special triangle?

1 habit:

Always check that the three final angles add to 180 degrees.
