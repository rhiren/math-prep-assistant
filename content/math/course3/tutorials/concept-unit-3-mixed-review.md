# Unit 3 Mixed Review

## Big Idea

Unit 3 turns slope into a complete language for linear relationships. A
relationship can be described by a table, graph, equation, two points, or a
story, and the same two questions keep coming back:

- What is the rate of change?
- What is the starting value?

## What To Review

- Proportional relationships are lines through `(0, 0)` and use `y = mx`.
- In `y = mx`, `m` is both the slope and the constant of proportionality.
- In `y = mx + b`, `m` is slope and `b` is the y-intercept.
- Slope can be found from points, tables, graphs, equations, or contexts.
- The y-intercept is the `y` value when `x = 0`.
- Different representations can describe the same line.

## Worked Example

A table has points `(0, -2)`, `(1, 1)`, and `(2, 4)`.

The slope is `3` because `y` increases by 3 whenever `x` increases by 1.

The y-intercept is `-2`, because the table includes `(0, -2)`.

So the equation is:

`y = 3x - 2`

## Representation Checklist

| Given | First move | What to write down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Graph | locate two clear points | slope and y-intercept |
| Table | compare two rows | constant rate of change |
| Equation | identify `m` and `b` | slope and starting value |
| Two points | use change in `y` over change in `x` | slope |
| Context | identify rate and initial amount | equation or comparison |

## Common Traps

- Do not call every straight line proportional.
- Do not use the first y-value as the y-intercept unless its x-value is 0.
- Do not compare rates before converting each representation to the same unit.
- Do not swap rise and run.

## Quick Self-Check

After solving, test one point in the equation or description you chose. If the
point works and the rate matches, the representation is probably consistent.
