# Similar Triangles and Constant Slope

## Big Idea

On a straight line, you can draw many right triangles by choosing two points and making a vertical rise and a horizontal run.

Those right triangles are similar. They may be different sizes, but they have the same angle where they sit on the line. Because they are similar, their matching side ratios stay equal.

That is why slope is constant on a line.

## What To Notice

- Rise is the vertical change.
- Run is the horizontal change.
- Slope is `rise / run`, or `change in y / change in x`.
- Larger and smaller slope triangles on the same line are similar.
- Similar triangles have proportional corresponding sides, so equivalent rise/run ratios give the same slope.

## Worked Example

One slope triangle on a line has rise `2` and run `3`.

A larger slope triangle on the same line has rise `6` and run `9`.

The triangles are similar because they share the same steep angle on the line and both have a right angle.

Compare the ratios:

`2 / 3 = 6 / 9`

Both ratios simplify to `2 / 3`, so the slope is constant.

## Why This Matters

Slope is not just a number from a formula. It is a repeated relationship.

If every slope triangle on a line is similar, then every pair of points on that line gives the same rise/run ratio. That lets you use any two clear points to find slope.

## Common Traps

- Do not compare rise from one triangle with run from another triangle.
- Do not switch rise and run.
- Do not think a larger slope triangle means a steeper line. If both rise and run scale by the same factor, the slope stays the same.
- Do not assume two triangles are similar just because they are both right triangles. They also need another matching angle.

## Strategy

| Step | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | What are the rise and run for each triangle? |
| 2 | Are the triangles on the same line or do they have matching acute angles? |
| 3 | Are corresponding rise and run lengths proportional? |
| 4 | Do the rise/run ratios simplify to the same value? |
| 5 | What does that tell me about the line's slope? |

## Apply It

A graph shows a line with points `(1, 2)`, `(4, 4)`, and `(7, 6)`.

From `(1, 2)` to `(4, 4)`, the rise is `2` and the run is `3`.

From `(4, 4)` to `(7, 6)`, the rise is also `2` and the run is also `3`.

The slope triangles match, so the slope is `2 / 3`.

If you used the larger move from `(1, 2)` to `(7, 6)`, the rise is `4` and the run is `6`. The ratio `4 / 6` also simplifies to `2 / 3`.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 parts:

- rise
- run
- slope ratio

2 reasons slope triangles are similar:

- they have right angles
- they share the same angle with the line

1 habit:

Use matching rise with matching run before simplifying.
