# Dilations on the Coordinate Plane

## Big Idea

A dilation resizes a figure. It can make the figure larger or smaller, but it keeps the same shape.

In this concept, the center of dilation is the origin. That means every point is pulled directly away from `(0, 0)` or pushed directly toward `(0, 0)`.

If the scale factor is `k`, then the coordinate rule is:

`(x, y) -> (kx, ky)`

Both coordinates are multiplied by the same scale factor.

## What To Notice

- A scale factor greater than `1` makes an enlargement.
- A scale factor between `0` and `1` makes a reduction.
- A scale factor of `2` doubles every distance from the origin.
- A scale factor of `1/2` cuts every distance from the origin in half.
- The image point and preimage point stay on the same ray from the origin when the scale factor is positive.
- Side lengths and perimeters multiply by the scale factor.
- Areas multiply by the square of the scale factor.

## Worked Example

Point `A` is at `(-3, 4)`. Dilate it from the origin by scale factor `2`.

Multiply both coordinates by `2`:

`(-3, 4) -> (-6, 8)`

So the image point is `A'(-6, 8)`.

The point moved farther from the origin, but it stayed in the same direction from the origin.

## Reduction Example

Point `B` is at `(8, -6)`. Dilate it from the origin by scale factor `1/2`.

`(8, -6) -> (4, -3)`

The image is `B'(4, -3)`. Since the scale factor is less than `1`, the point moved closer to the origin.

## Strategy

| Task | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Find an image point | Multiply both coordinates by the scale factor. |
| Find a preimage point | Divide both image coordinates by the scale factor. |
| Find a scale factor | Divide an image coordinate by the matching original coordinate. |
| Dilate a figure | Multiply every vertex by the same scale factor. |
| Check a dilation from the origin | The x-ratio and y-ratio should match for corresponding points. |

## Common Traps

- Do not add the scale factor to the coordinates. Dilations multiply.
- Do not multiply only one coordinate. Both `x` and `y` must use the same scale factor.
- Do not use the reciprocal unless you are working backward from image to preimage.
- Do not say area uses the same scale factor as side length. Area uses the scale factor squared.
- Do not assume dilations are rigid transformations. Dilations usually change size.

## Pause And Think

Suppose `C(6, 10)` is dilated from the origin by scale factor `1/2`.

The new x-coordinate is:

`6 * 1/2 = 3`

The new y-coordinate is:

`10 * 1/2 = 5`

So `C'` is `(3, 5)`.

Now reverse it. If `C'` is `(3, 5)` and the scale factor was `1/2`, then the original point was:

`(3 / 1/2, 5 / 1/2) = (6, 10)`

Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.

## Apply It

A triangle has vertices `P(1, 2)`, `Q(4, 2)`, and `R(2, 5)`. Dilate the triangle from the origin by scale factor `3`.

Multiply each coordinate by `3`:

- `P(1, 2) -> P'(3, 6)`
- `Q(4, 2) -> Q'(12, 6)`
- `R(2, 5) -> R'(6, 15)`

The side lengths are three times as long. The angle measures stay the same, so the image triangle is similar to the original triangle.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 things to remember:

- A dilation resizes a figure.
- From the origin, multiply both coordinates by the scale factor.
- Lengths multiply by `k`, but areas multiply by `k^2`.

2 questions to ask:

- Is the scale factor greater than `1` or between `0` and `1`?
- Am I finding the image or working backward to the preimage?
