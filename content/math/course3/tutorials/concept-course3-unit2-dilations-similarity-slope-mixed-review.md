# Unit 2 Mixed Review

## Big Idea

Unit 2 connects shape and steepness.

Dilations create similar figures. Similar figures have matching angles and proportional side lengths. Similar triangles explain why slope stays constant on a line: every slope triangle on the same line has the same rise/run ratio.

The unit moves from geometry to graphs, but the central idea stays the same:

Multiplicative relationships preserve structure.

## What To Review

- A dilation from the origin multiplies both coordinates by the scale factor.
- A scale factor greater than `1` enlarges a figure.
- A scale factor between `0` and `1` reduces a figure.
- Similar figures have congruent corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.
- AA similarity proves triangles are similar using two matching angle pairs.
- Slope triangles on the same line are similar, so slope is constant.
- Slope is `change in y / change in x`.
- On a graph, slope is `rise / run`.

## Worked Review Example

A line has two slope triangles.

The first triangle has rise `3` and run `4`.

The second triangle has rise `9` and run `12`.

Compare the ratios:

`3 / 4 = 9 / 12`

The ratios match, so the slope triangles are similar and the line has constant slope `3/4`.

This is the same kind of reasoning used with similar figures: matching lengths scale by the same factor.

## From Similarity To Slope

Similarity helps you trust that the slope does not depend on which two points you choose.

If you use a small slope triangle, maybe the rise/run is `2/5`.

If you use a larger slope triangle on the same line, maybe the rise/run is `6/15`.

The larger triangle is scaled by `3`, and `6/15` simplifies to `2/5`. The slope stays the same.

## Common Traps

- Do not add the scale factor to coordinates or side lengths. Dilations multiply.
- Do not use one angle pair alone to prove triangle similarity.
- Do not say AA proves congruence. It proves similarity.
- Do not compare non-corresponding sides.
- Do not switch rise and run when finding slope.
- Do not forget that negative slope means the line falls from left to right.

## Strategy

| If the problem asks about... | Think... |
| --- | --- |
| dilated point | multiply both coordinates |
| missing side | use corresponding sides and scale factor |
| similar triangles | check angle pairs or proportional sides |
| AA proof | find two matching angle pairs |
| constant slope | compare rise/run ratios |
| graph slope | count rise, then run |
| rate of change | divide output change by input change |

## Apply It

A triangle has side lengths that scale from `4` to `10`. The scale factor is:

`10 / 4 = 2.5`

If another side is `6`, the matching side is:

`6 * 2.5 = 15`

On a graph, a slope triangle might scale the same way. A rise/run of `6/4` and a larger rise/run of `15/10` both simplify to `3/2`.

That is the bridge from similar triangles to slope.
