# Unit 1 Mixed Review

## The big idea

Unit 1 connects real numbers, radicals, exponents, powers of ten, and scientific
notation. These ideas work together.

You should be able to:

- classify rational and irrational numbers
- convert repeating decimals to fractions
- evaluate square roots and cube roots
- approximate irrational numbers
- compare real numbers
- use exponent rules, including zero and negative exponents
- write and compute with scientific notation

## Real numbers

Rational numbers can be written as fractions. Their decimals terminate or
repeat.

Irrational numbers cannot be written as fractions. Their decimals do not
terminate or repeat.

`√49 = 7`, so it is rational.

`√50` is irrational because 50 is not a perfect square.

## Repeating decimals

Use place value or algebra to convert repeating decimals.

`0.272727... = 27/99 = 3/11`

## Roots and estimates

Square roots undo squaring. Cube roots undo cubing.

`√81 = 9`

`∛(-64) = -4`

To estimate, use benchmark perfect squares:

`64 < 70 < 81`, so `8 < √70 < 9`.

## Exponents

Positive exponents show repeated multiplication.

`x³ × x⁵ = x⁸`

`(x²)³ = x⁶`

Zero and negative exponents extend the same rules:

`a⁰ = 1` for `a ≠ 0`

`a⁻³ = 1/a³`

## Scientific notation

Scientific notation has the form:

`a × 10ⁿ`

where `1 ≤ a < 10`.

`8,200,000 = 8.2 × 10⁶`

`0.000045 = 4.5 × 10⁻⁵`

For multiplication, multiply coefficients and add exponents.

For division, divide coefficients and subtract exponents.

For addition and subtraction, match powers of ten first.

## Review strategy

1. Identify the number type or operation.
2. Use benchmark values before estimating.
3. Apply exponent rules only when the bases or powers match correctly.
4. Normalize scientific notation at the end.
5. Check whether the result is reasonable in size.
