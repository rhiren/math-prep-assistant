# Cube Roots and Perfect Cubes

## The big idea

Cubing and taking a cube root are inverse operations.

`4³ = 64`, so `∛64 = 4`.

A **perfect cube** is the product of an integer used as a factor three times.

`5³ = 5 × 5 × 5 = 125`

## What a cube root means

`∛216` asks:

> What number multiplied by itself three times equals 216?

The answer is `6`, because `6 × 6 × 6 = 216`.

## Negative cube roots are real

An odd number of negative factors gives a negative product:

`(-4)³ = -64`

Therefore:

`∛(-64) = -4`

This differs from square roots. A negative number has a real cube root, but it
does not have a real square root.

## Cube-root equations

`x³ = 125` has one real solution:

`x = 5`

Unlike `x² = 25`, which has two real solutions, a cube equation of this form
has one real solution because positive and negative numbers have different
cubes.

## Perfect cube or not?

Compare a number with nearby perfect cubes.

`4³ = 64`

`5³ = 125`

Since `64 < 90 < 125`, the number `90` is not a perfect cube and:

`4 < ∛90 < 5`

## Fractions and decimals

If the numerator and denominator are perfect cubes:

`∛(27/64) = ∛27 / ∛64 = 3/4`

For decimals:

`∛0.008 = 0.2`

because:

`0.2 × 0.2 × 0.2 = 0.008`

## Geometry connection

The volume of a cube is:

`V = s³`

If a cube has volume `343 cubic centimeters`, then:

`s = ∛343 = 7 centimeters`

Cube roots recover an edge length from a cube's volume.

## Comparing cube roots

The cube function preserves order:

If `a < b`, then `a³ < b³`.

To compare `∛70` with `4`:

`4³ = 64`

Since `70 > 64`, `∛70 > 4`.

For negative values:

`(-5)³ = -125` and `(-4)³ = -64`

Since `-100` lies between `-125` and `-64`:

`-5 < ∛(-100) < -4`

## Common mistakes

- Multiplying by 3 instead of using three equal factors. `4³` means
  `4 × 4 × 4`, not `4 × 3`.
- Treating cube roots like square roots and writing two answers. `x³ = 8` has
  the single real solution `x = 2`.
- Saying a negative number has no real cube root. Every real number has one
  real cube root.
- Confusing units: volume uses cubic units, while an edge length uses ordinary
  units.
- Assuming `∛(a + b) = ∛a + ∛b`. This is not generally true.

## Quick practice

1. Find `∛512`.
2. Find `∛(-216)`.
3. Solve `x³ = 729`.
4. Between which integers does `∛200` lie?
5. A cube has volume `1000 in³`. Find its edge length.

### Answers

1. `8`
2. `-6`
3. `x = 9`
4. Between `5` and `6`
5. `10 in`
