# Parallel Lines and Transversals

## Big Idea

When a line crosses two parallel lines, it creates angle patterns that are predictable.

The crossing line is called a transversal. If the two crossed lines are parallel, then some angle pairs are equal and some angle pairs add to 180 degrees.

This lesson is about recognizing those angle pairs and using the relationships to find missing angle measures.

## What To Notice

- Parallel lines never meet.
- A transversal crosses two or more lines.
- Vertical angles are across from each other and are equal.
- Corresponding angles are in matching positions and are equal when the lines are parallel.
- Alternate interior angles are inside the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal, and are equal.
- Alternate exterior angles are outside the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal, and are equal.
- Same-side interior angles are inside the parallel lines, on the same side of the transversal, and add to 180 degrees.

## Worked Example

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One angle measures 68 degrees.

The corresponding angle at the other intersection also measures 68 degrees because corresponding angles are equal when the lines are parallel.

The same-side interior angle next to it measures:

180 - 68 = 112

So the equal angle is 68 degrees, and its supplementary same-side interior partner is 112 degrees.

## Strategy

| Angle pair | Where to look | Relationship when lines are parallel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vertical angles | Across an intersection | Equal |
| Corresponding angles | Same relative position at each intersection | Equal |
| Alternate interior angles | Inside the lines, opposite sides of the transversal | Equal |
| Alternate exterior angles | Outside the lines, opposite sides of the transversal | Equal |
| Same-side interior angles | Inside the lines, same side of the transversal | Add to 180 degrees |
| Linear pair | Adjacent angles forming a straight line | Add to 180 degrees |

## Memory Anchors

Equal angle pairs:

- vertical
- corresponding
- alternate interior
- alternate exterior

Supplementary angle pairs:

- same-side interior
- linear pair

When you see parallel lines, first decide whether the relationship is equal or adds to 180. Then solve.

## Common Traps

- Do not assume every pair of angles is equal.
- Do not mix up alternate interior and same-side interior.
- Do not forget that same-side interior angles add to 180 degrees.
- Do not use corresponding angle rules unless the lines are parallel.
- Do not subtract from 90 unless the problem gives a right angle.
- Do not ignore vertical angles at the same intersection.

## Pause And Think

Suppose two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, and an interior angle is 125 degrees.

The alternate interior angle is also 125 degrees.

The same-side interior angle on the same side of the transversal is:

180 - 125 = 55

The key is choosing the relationship before doing arithmetic.

## Apply It

A student says, "The missing angle is 40 degrees because it looks acute."

That is not enough. A diagram can be misleading.

A stronger explanation is:

The missing angle is alternate interior to a 62 degree angle. Since the lines are parallel, alternate interior angles are equal. Therefore, the missing angle is 62 degrees.

Or, if the missing angle is same-side interior with a 62 degree angle:

180 - 62 = 118

So the missing angle is 118 degrees.
