# Missing Side Lengths in Similar Triangles

## Big Idea

Similar triangles have the same shape. Their corresponding angles match, and their corresponding side lengths are proportional.

That proportional relationship lets you find a missing side length.

The key is not just "make a proportion." The key is making a proportion with matching sides in the same order.

## What To Notice

- Corresponding sides are sides in the same position in similar triangles.
- A scale factor connects every pair of corresponding sides.
- To go from a smaller triangle to a larger triangle, multiply by the scale factor.
- To go from a larger triangle back to a smaller triangle, divide by the scale factor.
- Perimeters scale the same way side lengths do.

## Worked Example

Triangle A is similar to Triangle B.

A side of `4` units in Triangle A matches a side of `10` units in Triangle B.

Another side in Triangle A is `6` units. Let `x` be the matching side in Triangle B.

Find the scale factor:

`10 / 4 = 2.5`

Now multiply the matching side:

`6 * 2.5 = 15`

So `x = 15` units.

## Proportion Method

You can also write a proportion:

`4 / 10 = 6 / x`

This keeps the same order: Triangle A side divided by Triangle B side.

Cross multiply:

`4x = 60`

`x = 15`

Both methods work because they are using the same corresponding-side relationship.

## Working Backward

If the scale factor from Triangle A to Triangle B is `3`, and a side in Triangle B is `21` units, then the matching side in Triangle A is:

`21 / 3 = 7`

Use division when you are moving backward from image to original.

## Common Traps

- Do not compare a short side to a long side unless they actually correspond.
- Do not add the difference between one pair of sides to every other side.
- Do not flip one ratio but not the other. Keep the same order on both sides of the equation.
- Do not use area scale factor for side lengths.
- Do not assume the missing side is in the larger triangle; read which triangle the question asks about.

## Strategy

| Step | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Which sides correspond? |
| 2 | Am I going from original to image or image to original? |
| 3 | What scale factor connects a known pair? |
| 4 | Should I multiply or divide to find the missing side? |
| 5 | Does my answer make sense for the larger or smaller triangle? |

## Apply It

A tree and a student form similar right triangles with their shadows.

The student is `5` feet tall and casts an `8` foot shadow. The tree casts a `20` foot shadow.

The shadow scale factor is:

`20 / 8 = 2.5`

The tree height is:

`5 * 2.5 = 12.5`

The tree is `12.5` feet tall.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 checks:

- corresponding sides
- consistent ratio order
- multiply or divide direction

2 tools:

- scale factor
- proportion

1 habit:

Always label which triangle each side comes from before solving.
