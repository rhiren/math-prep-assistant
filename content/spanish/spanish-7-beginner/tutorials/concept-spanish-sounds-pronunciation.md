# Spanish Sounds and Pronunciation

## Hook

Spanish is friendly to beginners because the letters usually keep the same sounds. Once you learn a few patterns, you can read many new words aloud even before you know every meaning.

## Big Idea

Spanish pronunciation is more consistent than English pronunciation. Start with clear vowels, listen for stress, and treat accent marks as helpful signs.

Memory anchor: **Say what you see, then check the stress.**

## Learn It

The five Spanish vowels are short and steady:

| Letter | Sound |
|---|---|
| a | like the a in father |
| e | like the e in met |
| i | like ee in see |
| o | like the o in open, but shorter |
| u | like oo in food |

Spanish words usually stress the next-to-last syllable if they end in a vowel, n, or s. They usually stress the last syllable if they end in another consonant. Accent marks show when the stress breaks the usual pattern.

## Build The Model

Use this read-aloud routine:

`word -> vowel sounds -> syllables -> stress -> meaning`

Example:

`amigo -> a-mi-go -> stress mi -> friend`

## Memory Anchors

- **Spanish vowels stay steady.**
- **Accent marks are clues, not decorations.**
- **Pronunciation practice is listening plus speaking.**

## Common Traps

- Trap: Saying Spanish vowels like English vowels.  
  Fix: Keep each vowel short and steady.
- Trap: Ignoring accent marks.  
  Fix: Let the accent mark tell you where the voice lands.
- Trap: Trying to sound fast first.  
  Fix: Sound clear first. Speed comes later.

## Pause And Think

- What are the five Spanish vowel sounds?
- Why are accent marks useful?
- How would you break `familia` into syllables?

## Apply It

Read these aloud slowly: `hola`, `amigo`, `gracias`, `familia`, `teléfono`.

Circle the vowel sounds first. Then mark the syllable that sounds strongest.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 things to remember:

- Vowels are steady.
- Stress follows patterns.
- Accent marks show stress.

2 habits to build:

- Read slowly and clearly.
- Repeat after audio or a teacher when possible.

1 explain-it prompt:

- Why is Spanish often easier to sound out than English?
