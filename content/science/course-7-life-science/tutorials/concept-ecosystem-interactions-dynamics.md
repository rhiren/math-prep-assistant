# Ecosystem Interactions and Dynamics

An ecosystem includes living and nonliving parts that interact. Organisms depend on resources such as food, water, space, shelter, sunlight, and nutrients.

## Interactions

Common interactions include:

- competition for limited resources
- predation between predator and prey
- mutualism, where both organisms benefit
- food-web relationships that show energy transfer

Changes in one population can affect other populations.

## Matter and Energy

Food webs show how energy moves from producers to consumers and decomposers. Matter cycles as organisms eat, grow, release waste, die, and decompose.

## Ecosystem Stability

Ecosystems can change after disturbances such as drought, fire, invasive species, or human development. Some ecosystems recover more easily than others, depending on biodiversity and available resources.

## Practice Focus

Use data and diagrams carefully. Look for the cause, the effect, and whether the evidence supports a change in population size, resource availability, or ecosystem stability.
