# Ecosystem Interactions and Dynamics

## Hook

A pond has algae, insects, small fish, and herons. One summer, a drought lowers the water level. The algae changes, insect numbers change, and fish have fewer places to hide.

One change can ripple through the ecosystem.

## Big Idea

Ecosystems are systems of living and nonliving parts. Organisms interact with each other and depend on resources.

Memory anchor: **One change can ripple.**

## Learn It

Organisms need resources such as food, water, space, shelter, sunlight, and nutrients.

Common interactions:

- **competition**: organisms need the same limited resource
- **predation**: one organism eats another
- **mutualism**: both organisms benefit
- **food-web relationships**: energy transfers as organisms eat and are eaten

## Build The Model

Use food webs and cause/effect chains:

`producer -> consumer -> predator -> decomposer`

Example ripple:

`drought -> fewer pond plants -> fewer insects -> less food for fish`

Matter cycles as organisms eat, grow, release waste, die, and decompose. Energy flows from producers to consumers and decomposers.

## Memory Anchors

- **Resources limit populations.**
- **Food webs show energy flow.**
- **One change can ripple.**
- **Biodiversity can support stability.**

## Evidence Rules

When reading ecosystem data, ask:

- What changed first?
- Which resource changed?
- Which population increased or decreased?
- Does the evidence show cause, or only a pattern?

## Common Traps

- Trap: "Only the directly affected organism changes."  
  Fix: Food-web effects can spread to other populations.
- Trap: "More predators always means more prey."  
  Fix: Predator and prey populations affect each other in patterns.
- Trap: "A food web shows matter only."  
  Fix: Food webs mainly show energy transfer through feeding relationships.

## Pause And Think

Answer these without looking back:

- What is competition?
- Why might fewer plants affect fish?
- What does a food web help you trace?

## Apply It

An invasive plant grows quickly and blocks sunlight from native pond plants. Insects that eat the native plants decrease.

The likely chain is: invasive plant blocks sunlight, native plants decrease, insects lose food, insect population decreases.

## Try At Home

Observe a small outdoor area for 10 minutes and make a mini interaction map: producer, consumer, decomposer clue, shelter, water, or light. Look without touching plants or animals.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 things to remember:

- Ecosystems include living and nonliving parts.
- Organisms interact through resources and feeding relationships.
- Changes can ripple through a food web.

2 traps to avoid:

- Do not stop at the first organism affected.
- Do not ignore nonliving factors like water, light, and space.

1 explain-it prompt:

- How can a drought affect more than one population in an ecosystem?
