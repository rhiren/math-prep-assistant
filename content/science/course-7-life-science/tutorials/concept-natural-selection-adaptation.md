# Natural Selection and Adaptation

## Hook

On dark tree bark, brown insects are harder for birds to see than pale insects. After many generations, the insect population has more brown insects than before.

The insects did not choose a new color. The environment made one existing variation more helpful.

## Big Idea

Natural selection happens when inherited variation affects survival in a particular environment. Over generations, helpful traits can become more common in a population.

Memory anchor: **Variation first, selection second.**

## Learn It

Natural selection needs three ideas:

- individuals in a population vary
- some variation is inherited
- the environment affects which traits help survival

An adaptation is an inherited trait that helps organisms in a particular environment. A trait that helps in one place may not help in another.

## Build The Model

Use this chain:

`variation -> environmental pressure -> survival differences -> trait pattern changes`

Example:

`some insects are brown -> dark bark hides brown insects -> birds eat fewer brown insects -> brown color becomes more common`

## Memory Anchors

- **Populations change; individuals do not evolve by choice.**
- **Variation first, selection second.**
- **Helpful depends on environment.**
- **Traits can have tradeoffs.**

## Evidence Rules

Strong natural selection evidence usually shows:

- a trait varies in a population
- the trait affects survival in an environment
- the trait pattern changes over generations

## Common Traps

- Trap: "Organisms choose traits because they need them."  
  Fix: Variation already exists; the environment affects which traits help.
- Trap: "One individual evolves during its life."  
  Fix: Populations evolve over generations.
- Trap: "Helpful means perfect."  
  Fix: Traits can help in one situation and hurt in another.

## Pause And Think

Answer these without looking back:

- Why must variation exist before selection?
- What changes over generations?
- Why might a helpful trait depend on the environment?

## Apply It

A bird population has different beak shapes. During a drought, only large tough seeds are common. Birds with deeper beaks crack those seeds more easily.

If deeper beaks become more common over many generations, the evidence supports natural selection.

## Try At Home

Use two spoon types to pick up two small safe objects, such as cotton balls and dry beans, for 20 seconds. Compare which tool works better in which "environment" and connect that to trait fit, not effort.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 things to remember:

- Natural selection acts on inherited variation.
- Environments affect which traits help.
- Populations change over generations.

2 traps to avoid:

- Do not say organisms choose new traits because they need them.
- Do not say one individual evolves during its life.

1 explain-it prompt:

- How can a drought make one beak trait more common over generations?
