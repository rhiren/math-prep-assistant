# Limiting Factors and Carrying Capacity

## Hook

Two jars start with the same number of sprouting seeds. One jar has enough space, water, and light. The other is crowded and dries out quickly. After a week, the crowded jar has fewer healthy sprouts.

Populations also depend on resources and limits.

## Big Idea

A limiting factor is something that restricts population growth, such as food, water, space, shelter, disease, or predators. Carrying capacity is the population size an environment can support over time.

Memory anchor: **Resources set limits.**

## Learn It

Populations can increase when resources are available. But growth does not continue forever.

Limits can include:

- not enough food or water
- not enough space or nesting sites
- more predators
- disease spreading more easily in crowded groups
- changes in weather or habitat

A graph near carrying capacity often rises, slows, and levels off or fluctuates.

## Build The Model

Use this chain:

`resource change -> survival or growth-rate changes -> population pattern changes -> new pattern on graph`

Example:

`less water -> fewer seedlings survive -> plant population stops increasing`

## Memory Anchors

- **Resources set limits.**
- **Growth slows near capacity.**
- **Graphs show population stories.**
- **One factor can change the whole system.**

## Evidence Rules

Strong explanations use:

- the population data pattern
- the resource or limiting factor
- the cause/effect link
- whether the pattern repeats over time

## Common Traps

- Trap: "A population can grow forever if it starts growing."  
  Fix: Resources and other limiting factors slow growth.
- Trap: "Carrying capacity is always one exact number."  
  Fix: It can fluctuate as conditions change.
- Trap: "Any population drop has the same cause."  
  Fix: Use evidence to identify the limiting factor.

## Pause And Think

- What are three possible limiting factors?
- What would a graph look like near carrying capacity?
- Why should a claim name the resource evidence?

## Apply It

A deer population rises for several years, then levels off as plant food becomes scarce. A strong explanation connects food availability to survival, growth rate, and the graph pattern.

## Try At Home

Plant a few fast-sprouting seeds in two small cups if supplies are available. Keep one cup less crowded than the other, give both the same light and water, and compare growth. Wash hands after handling soil or seeds.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 things to remember:

- Limiting factors restrict population growth.
- Carrying capacity depends on resources and conditions.
- Population graphs need evidence-based explanations.

2 traps to avoid:

- Do not assume populations grow forever.
- Do not guess the cause of a graph without evidence.

1 explain-it prompt:

- How can less food change the shape of a population graph?
