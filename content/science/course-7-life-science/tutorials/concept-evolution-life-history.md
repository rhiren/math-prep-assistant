# Evolution and Life History

## Hook

A fossil fish and a modern fish have similar body structures. A fossil whale has bones that look like parts of hind limbs. These clues are not random facts; they are evidence about past life and change over time.

## Big Idea

Scientists use evidence from fossils, body structures, DNA, and populations to explain common ancestry and change over time.

Memory anchor: **Evidence connects past and present life.**

## Learn It

The fossil record gives evidence about past life. Fossils can show when different organisms lived, how body forms changed, and when some groups disappeared.

Similar structures may provide evidence that organisms share common ancestry. DNA patterns can also show relatedness.

## Build The Model

Use this evidence chain:

`fossil or structure -> pattern across organisms or time -> claim about relatedness or change`

Example:

`similar limb bones -> same bone pattern in different animals -> evidence for common ancestry`

## Memory Anchors

- **Evidence connects past and present life.**
- **Fossils show clues from long ago.**
- **Shared structures can show relatedness.**
- **Patterns are stronger than single examples.**

## Evidence Rules

Strong evolution explanations use evidence across time or across groups.

| Evidence | What It Can Support |
|---|---|
| fossils in older and newer rock layers | change over time |
| similar bone structures | possible common ancestry |
| DNA similarities | relatedness patterns |
| trait frequency data | population change |

## Common Traps

- Trap: "An individual evolves during its lifetime."  
  Fix: Populations change over generations.
- Trap: "Organisms choose the traits they need."  
  Fix: Inherited variation already exists; the environment affects which traits help.
- Trap: "One fossil tells the whole story."  
  Fix: Strong evidence comes from patterns across fossils and other data.

## Pause And Think

Answer these without looking back:

- What kind of evidence can fossils provide?
- How can similar body structures support common ancestry?
- Why is a pattern across many fossils stronger than one fossil alone?

## Apply It

A fossil layer contains shell fossils in a place that is dry land today. This evidence can support the idea that the local environment was different in the past.

A stronger explanation would compare rock layers, fossil types, and other evidence.

## Try At Home

Make three paper "rock layers" and place different drawn fossils in each layer. Practice making careful claims from position evidence, then add a "fault" line and explain why the claim becomes more cautious.

## 3-2-1 Memory Check

3 things to remember:

- Fossils provide evidence of past life.
- Shared structures can support common ancestry.
- Multiple evidence patterns make claims stronger.

2 traps to avoid:

- Do not overclaim from one fossil.
- Do not use similar function alone as the only evidence.
